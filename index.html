Does Your Degree Decide Your Destiny?

Every year, tens of thousands of Australians graduate with a degree and wonder the same question:
what happens next?

This visualisation follows Australian graduates from enrolment through to entry into the workforce, comparing fields of study, geographic movement, and the truth behind institutional prestige.

The Pipeline: What We Study vs. Where We Go

Enrolments don't dictate outcomes. While business and management and society and culture remain the largest cohorts, the real growth story has been in information technology, which surged 37.7% in 2023 following the pandemic tech boom.

Yet, graduating in a booming field does not guarantee an easy start.

When those large cohorts enter the workforce, many hit a wall. Both IT and creative arts see significant shares of graduates failing to secure full-time work or transition into further study right out of the gate. Conversely, education graduates are absorbed immediately into the workforce, plugging national shortages.

However, short-term struggles are rarely permanent. After three years, the landscape shifts dramatically.

IT graduates recover to achieve a 90.5% full-time employment rate, leaping ahead of engineering. Even creative arts nearly doubles its employment rate, proving that a slow start is not the end of a career trajectory.

The Value of a Degree

A high employment rate does not automatically equate to a high salary. While education offers supreme job security, its starting salary is eclipsed by health, which boasts the highest median starting pay ($85,375) despite lower initial full-time employment figures due to internship years.

Meanwhile, the massive graduating cohorts of business and society and culture face intense market saturation, keeping their median starting salaries suppressed below $76,000.

But a salary at one point in time is only part of the picture. Over three years, financial gaps widen. Engineering and IT salaries explode by over $30,000, while education stagnates, dropping from second to fifth place in overall pay.

Furthermore, within these fields, traditional gender disparities emerge. The pay gap points one way: men's median salary is higher in seven out of the eight fields. Natural and physical sciences has the largest gap at $5,200.

The only reversal is creative arts, where women earn slightly more, though within the lowest-paid industry overall.


How Has It Changed?

Over the last decade, the extremes have remained fixed: health has held the highest median graduate salary every year since 2015, and creative arts has reliably held the lowest.

The middle of the pack, however, is highly volatile. Engineering recently overtook education for second place, while science plummeted from fourth in 2018 to seventh by 2023.

Beyond salary rankings, the broader job market itself is shifting. Finding full-time work became noticeably harder for most fields between 2022 and 2025.

The share of graduates failing to secure full-time work rose in seven out of eight fields, with IT facing the steepest climb, jumping 11.3 percentage points as tech hiring cooled post-pandemic.


The Geography of Opportunity

Opportunities aren't just bound by what you study, but where you study. Relative to population, the ACT is a massive outlier, producing 2,645 graduates per 100,000 residents, more than double the rate of Queensland.

This density isn't purely organic; it's heavily influenced by domestic student migration.

Mapping the net flow of domestic students reveals a distinct northern migration.

Southern Australia and Tasmania see major student relocation, as students head to larger and more prestigious institutions along the eastern coast.

Queensland and Victoria are particularly attractive to interstate students. They act as sponges, absorbing students who are chasing lifestyle changes or specific educational offerings that their local institutions can't match.


Does Institutional Prestige Pay Off?

Click to highlight a university group

Australian students are often sold on the prestige of the Group of Eight (Go8) universities. However, mapping median salaries across top institutions tells a different story.

Curtin University (a non-Go8 institution in Western Australia) boasts a median graduate salary of $80,000, outperforming the prestigious University of Melbourne ($69,200).

This reality is echoed in full-time employment rates. The non-Go8 institutions consistently match or exceed their older, wealthier counterparts when it comes to placing graduates directly into jobs.

This suggests that local industry ties, mandatory placements, and specialised degrees often outweigh historical prestige in the modern job market.


Conclusion

The data is clear: your degree heavily influences your early destiny, but not always in the way you might expect.
Choosing a "safe" or prestigious path like Business at a Group of Eight university does not guarantee top-tier pay or immediate employment. Instead, outcomes are dictated by industry demand (like Education and Health), long-term career trajectories (like the massive three-year salary spikes in IT and Engineering), and geographic willingness to move.
